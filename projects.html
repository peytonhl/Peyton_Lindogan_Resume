01 / Flagship

Contour

Live in production Solo build Source private

A music ratings, reviews and streaming analytics platform. Rate albums and tracks, follow people whose taste you trust, and compare any two releases on era adjusted stream counts. Live on the web and inside an iOS shell.

The idea

Spotify had a few million users in 2008 and has more than 800 million now. A song that hit 500 million streams in 2016 did it on a much smaller platform, so raw stream counts quietly flatter recent releases. Contour normalizes every count against the platform's monthly active users at release time, which makes a 2012 record and a 2024 record comparable for the first time.

What it proves

That I can specify, build, ship and then operate a data product end to end. The interesting half is not the first release, it is keeping a catalog accurate against third party APIs that rate limit hard, change their terms, and strip fields without notice.

Engineering worth naming

  • A personalized feed with two collaborative filtering tiers reading precomputed neighbor tables, popularity normalized so mega hits cannot bridge dissimilar users.
  • A Redis persistent circuit breaker with half open recovery, written after a burst of requests earned a credential wide rate limit block that survived redeploys.
  • 27 background workers, several of which repair catalog data with no external calls at all, using agreement between editions the catalog already holds.
  • Health endpoints that report worker progress, not liveness, after a worker ticked for 44 hours doing no useful work and looked perfectly healthy.

02 / Argument

The Case Against Relaunches

Live app Public repo Solo build

An interactive data product arguing that Marvel's relaunch publishing model erodes long term franchise IP value, and that the comics pipeline is the upstream half of a thirty billion dollar film business. Eight sections, built like an executive brief rather than a notebook, and deployed so you can read the argument rather than the code.

Method

  • Welch's two sample t test comparing Rotten Tomatoes scores for films built on deep versus thin comic source material, with 95% confidence intervals and Cohen's d.
  • OLS regression fitted per relaunch volume on confirmed sales data, with the slope read as thousands of copies lost per issue.
  • Original datasets compiled from Comichron, Box Office Mojo and the ComicVine API into a committed SQLite snapshot, so the app runs with no keys.

What it found

  • Films built on deep source material score about 26 Rotten Tomatoes points higher on average than films built on thin material.
  • The Avengers line lost 56% of sustained readership across four relaunch cycles.
  • Average Amazing Spider-Man writer tenure has fallen to 2.4 years since 2018, compressing the runway that produced the adaptable material.

Why the caveats section matters

Every figure is labelled confirmed, estimate or publisher estimate, and the appendix says plainly where the argument is weakest: about thirty films is a small sample, source depth classification is my judgment, and correlation here does not establish that the comics caused the film quality. An argument that hides its weak points is not an argument, it is a pitch.

What confidentiality costs public fisheries data

Berkeley MIDS DATASCI 200 Team of 3

California publishes commercial fishery landings from roughly fifty thousand receipts a year, then suppresses any record representing fewer than three fishermen, vessels or businesses. This analysis takes California Halibut from 2015 to 2025 and asks what that rule actually costs a researcher.

The argument

Suppression is not evenly spread. It concentrates by port, gear type and use category, so published totals read lower than true statewide landings and fine grained analysis quietly becomes unreliable. The final section simulates progressively broader geographic groupings to show that a slightly coarser aggregation would preserve the same privacy while returning most of the dataset's usefulness.

Interpretability of head coaching impact in the NBA

Independent Team of 3 2024

How much of a team's performance can be attributed to the head coach once advanced player statistics are accounted for. Data cleaning and modeling in R, written up as a full report.

Why it is still here

Nobody assigned it. Three of us wanted to know how much of a team's record actually belongs to the coach, so we went and checked. It is also the earliest work where I cared more about whether a model could be explained than whether it fit, and that preference is the throughline to everything above it on this page.
