Career ladder / 4 rungs

A sequence of problems I want to solve.

Not a sequence of titles. Each rung below is somewhere I think I can contribute, and each one exists because of what it makes possible on the rung after it. I wrote this to be argued with, which is the point of publishing it.

Back to experience Where I want pushback

What I am aiming at

More minimum emission megawatts reaching the grid, and every operator able to move at the same speed getting them there regardless of how large its technology organization is.

Agencies able to spend their time deciding rather than processing. That is the outcome the four rungs below are ordered toward, and it is the test I would apply to any of them: does this get more clean capacity connected, faster, for operators who cannot buy their way there.

Rung 1

Systems that were never built to talk to each other are being asked to inform the same difficult decision

Problem

Operational telemetry and financial planning grew in separate systems, for good reasons at the time. The consequence is that forecasts for operational and capital plans are built on a heavily caveated picture of the generation fleet. Connecting those two worlds makes the decisions on top of them measurably more accurate.

Capability

Designing and owning a production data product at fleet scale, from ingestion through the medallion layers to the surface where planning happens. Knowing what breaks when operational data moves, and what it takes to keep it trustworthy on a daily cadence.

What it sets up

Delivering this is a proof that operational and financial data can be joined into a product that can answer large scale budgetary questions, and that the accuracy gain is real enough to measure. It also gives me a repeatable pattern for the rest of the map, because every problem below is another version of two systems that were never designed to talk to each other being asked to inform one decision.

Rung 2

Policy is the loudest lever for faster interconnection, but there are others we can pull in the meantime

Problem

Interconnection and permitting reform is necessary and it is happening, but it moves on its own timeline and its outcomes are hard to predict. Alongside it, there is work available to utilities and developers right now that does not require anyone's approval: how study packages and filings get assembled, how the underlying data gets prepared, how quickly a revision can be turned around. Those levers are less visible than policy, and they are exactly the places where I think I can make meaningful change.

Capability

Building the assembly layer for the data and documents these processes consume, so producing a package becomes a repeatable workflow.

What it sets up

The skill here is turning a document and data heavy process into a repeatable workflow. That is the same skill the later rungs need at larger scale.

Rung 3

Reviewers are staffed for judgment and spend most of their time on assembly

Problem

Regulatory and permitting review is human judgment sitting on top of a large volume of retrieval, comparison, and consistency checking. Queues also carry projects at very different levels of maturity, which adds processing load without adding decisions.

Capability

Defining a product on an unstructured regulatory corpus and setting the standard for when machine output is ready to put in front of a person. The most important part to me is how we evaluate such important decisions: error tolerance, escalation rules, and what a reviewer needs to be shown before they will sign. Automate up to the decision, preserve the record, and leave the signature for the person accountable for it.

What it sets up

Setting an evaluation standard in one process is what makes the approach portable. The specifics differ across agencies and jurisdictions, but the underlying question of when machine output is ready for a person does not.

Rung 4

The operators who would benefit most are the least able to implement it

Problem

The components exist in pieces across vendors and internal teams. What does not exist yet is a system shaped around how these institutions procure, review, and defend decisions.

Capability

Building what is needed of a full loop, from what gets built to who pays for it to how it holds up in front of whichever body is doing the reviewing, whether that is a state commission, a federal agency, a system operator, or a local permitting authority.

What it sets up

Nothing, and that is the point. This is the rung the others are for.

Where I want pushback

This is a set of bets, not a plan I am confident in. Three questions I would rather be wrong about early.

  • Does this pathway make sense, or is the connection between rungs weaker than I think?
  • Is anything out of order, and should there be more rungs or fewer?
  • Anything you would push back on, or would approach differently?
Tell me where this is wrong
