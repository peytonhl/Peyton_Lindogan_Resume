Why energy

Modernizing energy systems shapes how efficiently power is delivered, how costs are managed, and how feasible it becomes to integrate cleaner generation at scale.

I spend my time on reporting architecture, integration problems and large transformation programs. I also stay as close as I can to the business side: policy developments, operational realities, and the long term direction of the sector. I treat the technical work as a lever rather than the goal. What it moves is a reliable future grid.

The part I am most interested in is the practical side of the energy transition, which means improving the underlying infrastructure so cleaner and more resilient power becomes economically and operationally viable. That is a slower and less visible problem than the one that gets written about, and it is the one that decides whether the rest of it works.

I keep a longer view of that as a sequence of problems rather than a sequence of titles, written down so it can be argued with.

What I am actually good at

Three claims. Each one has a delivery behind it, not an adjective.

I prototype, then hand off

I build the data model myself in Databricks and put a working version in front of the business early. Engineering hardens what survives contact. That order matters: it keeps scope arguments grounded in something people have already used.

Proof: forecast-to-actuals automation across 4 core models, replacing a manual 8 month budgeting cycle for FP&A and the Power Generation Division.

I make the call

When the team surfaces competing technical approaches I pick one, selecting for fit to the use case rather than deferring the decision. That includes cutting my own scope: I killed an integration once vendor escalation outweighed its value, redirected to a path already proven in testing, and kept the decision reversible.

Proof: brokered one committed delivery timeline between two business units with competing priorities, and presented delivery and data provenance to CEO level reports.

I work the whole chain

Generation operations, energy trading, distributed energy, ERP financials, enterprise data strategy. Four rotations and two roles across the same company means I can read a requirement from a trading desk and a month end close team without needing either one translated for me. I stay close to the business side on purpose: policy, operational reality, and where the sector is going.

Proof: 19 business units negotiated onto a single committed reporting scope; 20+ end to end trading processes mapped; a 42 MW virtual power plant owned end to end.
